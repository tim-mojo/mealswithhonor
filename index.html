About Us

More than a meal at the door

Meals with Honor began with four volunteers, one route, and a borrowed church kitchen. We still work the same way: dependable meals, the same faces week after week, and no hoops to jump through to receive either. For the veterans and neighbors we serve, the knock at the door matters as much as the food.

What We Do

Three ways we show up

Meals with Honor fights food insecurity and isolation in our community by delivering meals, building volunteer relationships, and creating spaces where people managing PTSD find connection and support.

Lunches & Dinners

Hot, balanced meals delivered on a set weekly schedule, with dietary needs accommodated at intake. No cost, and no paperwork after the first visit.

Volunteer Support

The same volunteer returns to the same door each week. Routes are built around relationships, not efficiency, so nobody is a stranger at the doorstep.

Social & Peer Support

Weekly community tables and peer-led groups where veterans managing PTSD can sit with people who already understand the shorthand.

Who We Serve

Neighbors, not case numbers

Veterans managing PTSD

Isolation compounds everything else. Our tables and peer groups are built to be low-pressure and predictable — come when you can, leave when you need to, and nobody asks you to perform being fine.

Households facing food insecurity

For veterans on fixed incomes, a hard month can mean skipped meals. We deliver on a schedule you can plan around, and we keep delivering for as long as it helps.
