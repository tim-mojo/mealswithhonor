About Us

A meal is the easiest excuse to check on someone.

Meals with Honor began with one route and four volunteers. The meals were always the point of entry — the visit was always the point.

Our Story

Started at a kitchen table

A neighbor noticed that the veteran two doors down had stopped coming outside. Groceries were the obvious problem. The quieter problem was that nobody had knocked in three weeks.

That first winter, four volunteers ran one route out of a borrowed church kitchen. The meals were reliable, which mattered. What mattered more was that the same faces came back, week after week, until the door opened faster.

We’ve grown since, but the design has not changed: consistent meals, consistent people, and no process that makes anyone prove they deserve either.

How We Work

Four commitments we don't bend on

The same volunteer, the same door

Routes are assigned for continuity rather than shortest driving time. It costs us efficiency and buys back the thing the program actually runs on.

No means test after intake

One conversation at the start, then meals. We don't ask anyone to re-qualify for dinner every quarter.

Food people actually want

Dietary needs are recorded at intake and honored. A meal nobody eats is not a meal delivered.

Peers lead the peer work

Our support tables are led by veterans. Staff set the room up and then get out of the way.

Come see a route

The fastest way to understand the program is to ride along on one. We schedule observer seats most Tuesdays.
